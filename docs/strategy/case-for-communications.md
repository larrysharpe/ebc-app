# A case for Communications at Ebenezer

**Audience:** Pastor, Joint Board, ministry leaders  
**Purpose:** Agree that we need a small, named group responsible for the church’s public voice — then decide what to call it and who leads it.  
**How the team works:** People own the message. **AI sits on the team as the expert** — the always-available writer, researcher, and second set of eyes the small group leans on. Humans still decide what goes out; the pastor still guards doctrine and crisis.

---

## The problem (in plain terms)

Right now, **everyone communicates, and no one owns communication.**

Ministries mean well. They announce from the floor, post on social media, email the office, ask the pastor to say something Sunday morning, update the website “when someone has time,” and print bulletin notes that don’t always match what’s said from the pulpit.

What the congregation and the community experience:

| What they see | What it costs us |
|---------------|------------------|
| Different dates or details for the same event | Confusion, missed attendance, embarrassment |
| Website or Facebook quieter than church life really is | Guests think little is happening |
| Last-minute “please announce this” to the pastor | Pastor becomes the bulletin board |
| Weather or schedule changes that don’t reach everyone the same way | People show up (or stay home) for the wrong reason |
| Good ministry work that never gets shared clearly | Outreach and invitation suffer |

This is not a people problem. It is a **structure** problem. Media runs sound and livestream. The office answers the phone. Missionary does outreach on the ground. **Nobody’s job is “one clear message, on time, on the right channels.”**

---

## What we are *not* asking for

Be clear with leadership so this doesn’t sound like a takeover:

- **Not** replacing the pastor’s voice or authority on doctrine and major announcements  
- **Not** replacing Media Ministry (sound, camera, livestream, slides)  
- **Not** a big new budget or a full-time hire on day one  
- **Not** stopping ministries from caring about their events — only stopping **scattered** public messaging  
- **Not** letting a machine speak for the church without a person responsible  
- **Not** “technology for technology’s sake”

---

## What we *are* asking for

A **small Communications team** (call it ministry, committee, or team — the name can wait) whose only job is:

1. **Gather** what needs to be said (from ministries, calendar, office, pastor)  
2. **Shape** one clear version (bulletin, website note, in-service cue, social when needed)  
3. **Clear** ordinary items with the lead; **sensitive** items with the pastor  
4. **Send** it out on the right channels on time  
5. **Keep** the public face of Ebenezer consistent with who we are — *Putting the Family Back Together*

That is it. Same work we already do poorly because it is everyone’s side job.

---

## AI on the team — like a member, treated as the expert

A lead plus two or three helpers cannot also be full-time copywriters, researchers, editors, and social strategists. So the team **makes room for AI the way it would for a gifted volunteer who never sleeps** — and it **leans on AI’s strength: expertise at draft, structure, options, and catch-mistakes**.

| Role on the team | What they bring |
|------------------|-----------------|
| **Communications lead** | Owns the cadence, deadlines, and final ordinary approval |
| **Helpers** (bulletin / website notes / social) | Human judgment, church knowledge, relationships |
| **Pastor** | Doctrine, crisis, major vision — always human |
| **AI (team expert)** | First drafts, rewrites, channel variants, checklists, “what are we missing?”, calm second pass |

**How to treat AI like a teammate (not a gadget):**

- Give it a **seat at the table** — every weekly cycle starts with “ask the expert” before people stare at a blank page  
- **Lean on what it’s good at** — wording, options, consistency, reminders of gaps (quiet week, missing Zoom link, event next Sunday with no promo)  
- **Never pretend it has authority** — it proposes; the lead (or pastor) disposes  
- **Never feed it what doesn’t belong** — prayer requests, personal contact lists, giving amounts, pastoral notes  
- **Credit the humans** — when something ships, a person signed off; AI helped them get there faster and clearer  

Think of it as: *we hired the best writer/researcher we could never afford — and still kept a human supervisor.*

That is how a small team punches above its weight without burning out the office or interrupting the pastor every Sunday.

---

## Why “team / ministry” — not “just the office”

The church office already carries too much. Asking one staff person to also be writer, editor, social media, bulletin, and website for every ministry **guarantees** backlog and burnout.

A named team means:

- Ministries know **where** to send requests (one inbox / one person)  
- There is a **deadline** (e.g. mid-week for Sunday) everyone can respect  
- The pastor is asked to approve **what matters**, not every bake sale line  
- When someone is out sick, the work still has a home  
- AI has a **home team** to work for — not random people pasting church business into random tools  

Usher, Music, Media, Trustees — we already treat those as real ministries. **The church’s voice deserves the same dignity.**

## A picture of “after”

| Before | After |
|--------|--------|
| Five people announce five versions | One approved version, several channels |
| Pastor gets tapped Sunday morning | Requests in by a known deadline |
| Blank-page struggle every week | AI expert drafts; people edit and approve |
| Website drifts | Communications prepares copy; office or designee publishes |
| Social “whenever someone remembers” | Someone responsible, still under pastor for sensitive posts |
| Media asked to “just put something up” | Media gets **approved slide copy** from Communications |

Media still runs the board and the stream. Communications brings the **words**. AI helps write them; people stand behind them.

---

## Small start (so it feels safe)

Do not launch a twenty-person org. Ask leadership to approve **phase one**:

| Item | Proposal |
|------|----------|
| **Name** | Communications Team *(or Ministry — leadership chooses)* |
| **Size** | Lead + 2–3 helpers + **AI as the expert teammate** |
| **Authority** | Ordinary church news: team lead. Doctrine / crisis / politics / major vision: **Pastor**. AI: never the final word. |
| **Partners** | Church office · Media · ministry leaders (submit, don’t freelance publish) |
| **First 90 days** | Weekly announcement rhythm + accurate Sunday cues + one owner for public Facebook/YouTube *messaging* (not AV) — with AI drafts as the default first pass |
| **Success looks like** | Fewer conflicting announcements; pastor interrupted less; guests find correct times and events; team not drowning in blank pages |

If that works, grow. If it doesn’t, adjust. No permanent empire required to try.

## Talking points (when someone says “we’ve always done it this way”)

**“We’ve managed fine.”**  
We’ve managed — and we still lose people in the gaps. Fine for us is not always clear for a first-time guest.

**“That’s the office’s job.”**  
The office should not be the only writer for every ministry. Support them with a team, don’t dump more on them.

**“That’s Media.”**  
Media is production (sound, stream, slides). Words and timing are a different gift. They work together.

**“We don’t need another ministry.”**  
Then call it a **team** under the office or pastor. The need doesn’t change with the label.

**“I don’t want robots speaking for the church.”**  
Neither do we. AI is a **team expert** — drafts and options. A person always signs off. The pastor still owns doctrine and crisis. Nothing goes public on AI’s word alone.

**“Why involve AI at all?”**  
Because two or three volunteers cannot be world-class writers every week. Leaning on an expert teammate is stewardship — same hours, clearer message, less burnout.

---

## Decision we need from leadership

Please decide:

1. **Yes / no** — We will have a named Communications function (team or ministry).  
2. **Who** — Interim lead (even temporary) and pastor’s approval path for sensitive items.  
3. **Name** — “Team,” “Ministry,” or “Committee.”  
4. **First mandate** — Weekly announcements + public channel ownership (not AV), with AI treated as the team’s expert for drafts and gaps.  

Until those four are answered, org charts and software rollouts wait on the people decision.

## Alignment with who we already are

Our mission is **Putting the Family Back Together**. Families need clear communication at home; the church family needs it too. Our theme is **We’re Marching to Zion!** — marching together means the same directions, not five different maps.

Good teams have specialists. Communications’ specialist for *words and options* can be AI — supervised by people who love this church.
